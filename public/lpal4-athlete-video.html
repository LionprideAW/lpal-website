Your intro video

Before your walkout in Cologne, a short film about you plays on the big screen in the hall and live on KICK. Most of the people watching have never heard your name. After your film they should know who you are, what you do, and why they should cheer for you.

You film, we edit. This page tells you exactly what to film and how.

Before you press record

Camera setup

These settings are the same for every part. Bad sound or a vertical video cannot be fixed later, so check them before every recording.

1
4K at 30 fps

iPhone: Settings → Camera → Record Video → 4K at 30 fps. Android: in the camera app, open video settings and choose 4K / UHD, 30 fps. Or use a camera if you have one.

2
2× lens, back camera

On the phone, use the back camera and tap 2×. On a camera, use a lens around 30 mm. No selfie camera, no filters, no beauty mode, no music.

3
Stable camera

For the talking parts, put the phone on a tripod or lean it on books or a shelf at eye height. No shaky hand-held interviews.

4
Clear sound

Quiet room. Turn off TV, music, fans and washing machine. Phone not more than 2 m away from you, or use a clip-on microphone if you have one.

5
Good light

Light comes from the side or front (window or lamp). Never sit with a bright window behind you.

6
Clean the lens

Wipe the camera lens with a T-shirt before you start. A dirty lens makes everything blurry.

7
Lock focus

Tap and hold on your face (or the main subject) on the screen until it says AE/AF lock, so the picture doesn't change while you talk.

8
Many takes, long clips

Wait 3 seconds after pressing record before you talk, and 3 seconds at the end. Say important sentences 2–3 times. More material = better edit.

Language
The whole video is best in English. Part 1 must be in English. If your English isn't good enough for the rest, speak your own language, but slowly and clearly, so we can translate it easily.
What to wear
A Lion Pride T-shirt if you have one. Otherwise a normal T-shirt in one dark colour (black, dark grey, navy). A small brand logo like Nike or Adidas is fine. No big prints or text.
1

Part 1 · in English

Who you are

Look into the lensEnglishChest upDark or plain background

This is the opening of your film. You look straight into the camera lens (not at the screen) and introduce yourself in English: your name, your biggest wins in armwrestling, and who you fight at LPAL 4. This part must be in English, even if you speak your own language in the other parts.

Script My name is [first and last name]. I'm from [city, country]. I'm [your biggest title, e.g. a multiple-time national champion] in armwrestling, and I'm coming to Cologne, Germany, to compete against [opponent] at LPAL 4.
Example: “My name is John Smith. I'm a multiple-time German national champion in armwrestling, and I'm coming to Cologne, Germany, to compete against Max Phillips at LPAL 4.”
  • Name your best titles: national, European or world championships, world cups and big tournaments. If there are videos of those tournaments, mention them and paste the links into the text file in your folder.
  • Say it 4–5 times: calm, then with more energy. Pick your best one later, we take the strongest.
  • Learn it by heart so you don't read it. An accent is totally fine.
2

Part 2 · the interview

Your story

Look to the side, not into the lensBlack backgroundAt homeFilmed from the side

This is the heart of the film. Imagine nobody knows who you are. Tell your story: where you come from, what you do every day, and how you became an armwrestler. Someone (a friend, partner, family member) sits next to the camera and asks you the questions. You look at that person, not into the lens.

How to get a black background

  • Film in the evening, or close the curtains.
  • Turn off the ceiling light. Use one lamp or a window on one side of you.
  • Sit far away from the wall (1.5 m or more), so the background falls into darkness.
  • Wear a dark T-shirt in one colour (see “What to wear” above).

Questions to answer

The interviewer reads them out. Answer in full sentences that also make sense without the question (“I work as a carpenter in…”, not just “Carpenter.”). Best in English; if you can't, speak your own language slowly and clearly. Take your time; 10–15 minutes of talking is perfect.

  1. Who are you? Where are you from, and where do you live now?
  2. What do you do for work or school? What exactly does your job look like?
  3. Describe one normal day of yours, from the moment you wake up until you go to bed.
  4. How and when did you start armwrestling? Who brought you to the sport?
  5. What are your biggest wins? Name the tournament and the year (we will look for the footage).
  6. What is your hardest loss or toughest moment, and what did you learn from it?
  7. What would people never guess about you? Family, kids, pets, other sports, hobbies, a funny story.
  8. What does armwrestling mean to you? Why do you do it?
Important: everything you talk about, we need to see. If you mention your job, school, your kids, your dog or your car garage, film it or send photos of it (Part 3). A story without pictures can't be used.

Part 3 · no talking needed

Your day

Work or schoolHomeHobbiesVideos + photos

While you tell your story, we show your life. Ask a friend or colleague to film you during a normal day: at work or school, on the way there, at home. Don't look into the camera, just do what you always do.

WideThe whole place: your workshop, office, classroom, building from outside.
MediumYou working, from the waist up. Film from the side or slightly behind.
Close-up / handsHands on tools, keyboard, steering wheel, books. Details tell the story.
Low angleCamera on the floor: feet walking in, arriving at work, getting out of the car.
Follow from behindWalk behind you, camera at shoulder height: walking into work, the gym, the hall.
PhotosOld photos: childhood, first tournament, family, your team, medals.
  • Every clip 10–30 seconds, camera still or moving slowly. Film each thing from 2–3 different angles.
  • At least 5–10 clips from work or school. If you can't film at work, film the outside of the building, your work clothes, your tools, the drive there.
  • Home and free time too: cooking, kids, pets, other sports, your car, whatever is “you”.
4

Part 4 · in the gym

Training

3–4 exercisesSparring at the tableVery close + far away

Show how hard you work. Film 3–4 of your typical armwrestling exercises and sparring at the table with your training partners. Every exercise from several angles: some very close, some far away.

Examples from LPAL events. In the gym it works the same way.

  • Each exercise: 1 close clip, 1 medium, 1 wide. Clips 10–30 seconds.
  • Sparring: film from the side of the table, from behind your opponent (your face to the camera), and very close on the hands.
  • Extra: chalking up, wrapping/straps, the walk into the gym, a shout after a heavy set. Real sounds are welcome.
  • Phone horizontal, on a tripod or held steady with two hands. Lots of light in the gym helps.

Part 5 · same setup as Part 2

The match

Look to the sideLast sentence into the lensBlack background

Now talk about your fight at LPAL 4. Be confident and be yourself. Respect is fine, a little trash talk is fine too, but no insults about family, country or religion.

Questions to answer

  1. Who is your opponent at LPAL 4, and what do you know about him / her?
  2. What is your opponent strong at? Where is he / she weak?
  3. How will you win this match? (No secrets needed, just your feeling.)
  4. How are you preparing for this match?
  5. What does it mean to you to fight on a big stage in Cologne, live on KICK?

Then look into the lens and finish with one strong sentence. Some ideas, say it in your own words:

Closing lines (pick one or make your own) “[Opponent], I respect you, but on January 9th in Cologne the win stays with me.”
“I didn't train this hard to lose. See you at the table in Cologne.”
“[Opponent], be ready for the toughest match of your life.”
“[Opponent], you will feel power you have never felt before.”
“[Opponent], with [your country] behind me, I can't be stopped. Prepare to lose.”

Part 6 · someone else talks about you

Your training partner

Partner looks to the sideIn the gym1–2 minutes

Pick one training partner (or your coach) who knows you well. He or she talks about you: how hard you train, how long you know each other, and why you will win. While they talk, we show your training and match footage.

Script for your partner My name is [name], I'm [athlete]'s training partner. We have known each other for [number] years and we train together [how often].

Then they answer

  1. How hard does [athlete] train? Tell one story from training.
  2. What makes him / her dangerous at the table?
  3. What is he / she like outside the gym?
  4. Why will he / she beat [opponent] in Cologne?

How to film it

  • Same as Part 2: partner looks at the person asking, next to the camera, not into the lens.
  • Film it in the gym (a table or weights in the background is great), or at home with a dark background.
  • Quiet moment: no music in the gym while recording.
  • Phone horizontal, on a tripod, 2× lens, about 1.5–2 m away.
